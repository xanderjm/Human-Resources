Keep the upgrade structure.
Revise the survival curve.

12,520 seeded model runs found a workable endless card pool, but no tested numerical profile yet satisfies the intended 20–30-minute good-run target consistently. Simulations expose mechanical risks; they do not establish that the game is fun.

Duration target

Vampire Survivors’ standard-stage benchmark is about 30 minutes of game time (Xbox Wire). Brotato’s developer describes runs as under 30 minutes (official press kit). Temple Run is endless with no fixed completion time (Imangi press release); no credible average player duration was established here. Completed stages are not evidence of average failed-attempt length.

For Human Resources, aim for a satisfying 20–30-minute good run. Learning attempts should end sooner; exceptional builds and skilled piloting can continue beyond that. This is an initial design target, not a forced death timer. Menus and upgrade pauses add wall-clock time.

What was simulated

8,760 runs in the main comparison, 3,600 in a gentler-research follow-up and 160 longer follow-up runs. The main study includes eight automated selection policies, five candidate tunings, seven separate pressure/target-density/dodging sensitivity cases, 30/60-minute horizons and a 0.5s versus 1s timestep check. Sixteen traced replays are excluded from the total. Follow-ups reuse declared seed sets and are exploratory; they are not extra independent confirmation.

The model earns XP from active survival, captures and kills, draws three eligible cards at each earned level, applies ranks and evolutions, and resolves approximate military attacks, shields and repairs. Seeded worlds are paired across profile comparisons. It also passed twelve focused model tests. A separate card audit covered 3,000 drafts × 200 level-ups and 1,000 maxed-build tail draws.

Problems the study exposed

  1. Fixed-rarity upgrades starve rare modules. Gunship policies finished only 28% of the 50 rare modules found in the baseline holdout. The aggressive profile finished 97.7% of 44 found. Those percentages are conditional on discovery, not the chance to acquire a rare module. In the initial isolated-change study, easier owned-rank draws alone raised Gunship rare completion from 28.6% to 68.2%; other combined changes also affect the final result.
  2. Flat repeatable gains become small relative to an established build. The baseline Hybrid policy had 68.8% small later offers. Larger research reduced this to 9%, but raised 30-minute survival from 81.5% to 98.5%. Merely increasing endless stat gains creates a survival problem.
  3. Specialist payoff is weak under these assumptions. Baseline Hybrid and Collector policies averaged almost identical total captures (2,470.8 versus 2,470.5), yet Hybrid survived to 30 minutes more often (81.5% versus 62.5%). Collector captured faster per observed minute (87.4 versus 83.3), but lost much of that advantage through earlier deaths. The model assigns vehicle captures an arbitrary flat five score, so the final reward design could change this result.
  4. Rerolls trade frustration for more predictable rosters. Under the combined profile, Hybrid runs encountering an all-off-preference new-hardware offer fell from 29% to 2%. Their most common final roster rose from 37.5% to 58%. This is roster frequency for one preset bot, not measured human diversity or a proof that other builds are bad.
  5. The difficulty curve has cliffs. In the baseline 60-minute samples, 30 of 80 runs died between 34:00 and 35:30, coinciding with a scheduled boss window. All four policy medians sat near 35 minutes. The model suggests spreading escalation across encounters rather than relying on boss pressure. Timing correlation does not isolate the boss as the only cause.

Recommended first-playable candidate

  • Keep three random choices per level, six hardware slots and three incremental picks per module. Beam Splitter remains +1 beam per pick: 1 → 2 → 3 → 4; its earned evolution is a separately described unlock.
  • Keep rarity meaningful for finding new blueprints. Once installed, put subsequent ranks in the Common draw bucket while retaining the blueprint’s original rarity label. All cards remain random and distinct by family.
  • Provide two optional rerolls per run. Check in playtests whether this makes the same build too reliable.
  • Use fifteen-pick research bundles as an experimental starting point. Do not increase capped fire-rate/capture-speed research or immediate repair gains. Show the actual gain and resulting total on each card.
  • Retune late military pressure and capture-based sustain together before signing off the numbers. Difficulty follows active run time, independent of player level. No tested director adjustment is being declared final here.
  • Give capture specialists a visible score/resource payoff, then test whether their survival tradeoff is worth taking. Keep score separate from XP.

The machine-readable candidate overlay records these changes and unresolved decisions. The canonical catalogue has not been silently rewritten.

What remains unmeasured

These are offline encounter approximations. They do not execute the native game, simulate physical collision/lanes, measure input latency or test visual/audio feedback. Automated choices use fixed heuristic preferences, not optimal planning or human judgment. There is no measured “fun percentage.”

Movement currently improves dodge odds, target encounters and how quickly ordinary enemies leave the screen. If the actual runner separates forward scrolling from lateral movement, this can overvalue Vector Thrusters and alter the winning roster. Lasers and drones pool damage into the oldest threat, instead of using actual independent geometry; pierce/arc alignment and capture retention use statistical approximations. Bosses can leave after 90 seconds, so surviving one does not imply killing it. Vehicle rewards and encounter numbers are assumptions, not calibrated rules.

Results respond strongly to those assumptions. For baseline Hybrid, 30-minute survival ranged from 40% with novice dodging to 92.5% with expert dodging; separate military-pressure variants gave 42.5% to 97.5%. These are sensitivity experiments, not predictions of novice/expert players. Halving the timestep changed the survival outcome of 5% of Gunship samples and 0% of Hybrid samples; redrafting diverges with XP timing, and this is not a numerical convergence proof.

Hands-on checks for the first playable

Observe 8–12 people over several attempts, recording all offers, choices, active duration, causes of death and capture/kill rates. Check whether players understand one-pick gains without explanation; whether they can describe their build after five minutes; whether late choices prompt an actual decision; whether deaths feel avoidable; and whether they voluntarily start another run. Compare several viable builds and revisit the 20–30-minute target using observed attempts. These are proposed playtest criteria, not completed human research.
