# Invasion edition: reference study and design decisions

Research date: 24 October 2026. These are visual design interpretations of the
linked reference artwork and the media's premise. They are not claims about the
names of fonts used by those productions.

## What the first edition missed

The original pack used rounded, mostly monoline skeletons for almost every role.
Its main differences were stroke weight and lean. That supplied a coherent UI,
but the lettering did little to communicate invasion, panic or the dry joke in
the name Human Resources. The second edition gives the invaders, announcements,
administration and Earth locations different typographic voices.

## References

### Mars Attacks! — theatrical scale and camp menace

The poster artwork sets an exaggerated invasion against a forceful title. Its
appeal here is its theatrical, deliberately excessive treatment of an absurd
threat. My translation is a broad, heavy alphabet with flared stems, uneven
shoulders, off-axis counters and strong silhouettes. The new title is a filled
outline design; it is not the old rounded face with extra weight.

### Destroy All Humans! — the invader gets the joke

The official game places the player inside a satirical invasion of 1950s Earth.
Its title artwork and domineering presentation make the alien side feel
confident and overblown. The useful connection to this game is the contrast
between ridiculous abduction and the invader's matter-of-fact attitude. The
new title is called Hostile Takeover; the interface uses acquisition and
personnel language. The prototype phrases are original game-copy suggestions.

- [Official game site and title artwork](https://destroyallhumans.thqnordic.com/)
- [Publisher description on Nintendo UK](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Games/Nintendo-Switch-games/Destroy-All-Humans--1959779.html)

### Invader Zim — controlled awkwardness

The logo's elongated shapes, abrupt angles and uneven rhythm give it a specific,
unsettling comic voice. Panic Broadcast translates those broad principles into
new, narrow capitals with bent axes and blunt terminals. Its lowercase is
angular too. The letters remain identifiable and align predictably in a game;
the font does not randomize glyphs on each render.

- [Logo reference, TMDB](https://www.themoviedb.org/tv/3793-invader-zim/images/logos)
- [Official Netflix trailer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TDnKp83IOPA)
- [Netflix's description of Enter the Florpus](https://www.netflix.com/title/81091957)

### They Live — administration as the threat

The film's premise connects alien control with ordinary society and submission.
The useful lesson is the contrast between alarming content and impersonal
authority. Bureau gives the interface a restrained administrative voice, with
chamfered forms, clear numerals and a double-storey a. The chamfered construction
is an original choice for this game's alien equipment, rather than a claim
that it reproduces the film's typography.

- [Universal's official film page](https://www.universalpicturesathome.com/movies/they-live)
- [Licensed poster discussion by Mondo](https://mondoshop.com/blogs/news/they-live-poster-by-alan-hynes)

## The resulting system

| Typeface | Letterform decisions | Game role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HR Hostile Takeover Black | Original filled wedges, uneven cap silhouette, heavy shoulders, tilted counters | Main title, major results, promotional headlines |
| HR Panic Broadcast Bold | Narrow bent capitals, abrupt diagonals, blunt ends, angular lowercase | Short announcements and capture bonuses |
| HR Bureau Medium / SemiBold / Bold | Chamfered bowls, open spacing, double-storey a, slashed zero | Buttons, upgrade descriptions, score and settings |
| HR Earthling Signs Regular | Slab serifs on structural stems, sturdy angular forms | Farm notices and roadside signs |

Earthling Signs is an invented vernacular for this game's mundane Earth
locations. Its slab construction helps separate human spaces from alien
administration. It is not attributed to a particular film font.

## Direction for the actual game

Use Hostile Takeover as the dominant personality. Set the title in two lines,
with a solid ivory face and a restrained coral offset if useful. A separate
poster-style SVG lockup includes that optional treatment; the font remains a
clean, single-colour outline font. Keep navy, ivory, mint and coral from the
existing art direction.

Use Panic Broadcast briefly, where the game deserves an outburst. Keep upgrade
descriptions and HUD numbers in Bureau. Use Earthling Signs on a few readable
landmarks. The contrast between confident alien administration and ordinary
Earth signage supplies the humour; every screen need not shout.

## Provenance

The pack contains original vector drawings and generated font files. It does
not contain downloaded reference posters, franchise logos, screenshots or
traced letterforms. The external artworks remain references at their source
URLs; the package's MIT licence applies to the new font software and assets.
